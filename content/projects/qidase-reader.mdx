## The problem

The Divine Liturgy (Qidase) of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is prayed in Ge'ez, a language
most of the congregation doesn't speak. People follow along with printed books in Amharic or English,
and it's easy to lose your place, especially if you're a catechumen.

## What we built

Qidase Reader shows the liturgy side by side in Ge'ez, Amharic, English, and transliteration. Each
prayer is marked by who says it: priest, deacon, or congregation. It covers the Kidan, the Serate
Kidase, and the anaphoras (Fere Qidase). It runs on iOS, Android, and the web from one Expo codebase.

Before a service, you enter the day's readings once and the app slots them into the right places. A
full-screen presentation mode then puts the text on a projector, one block at a time, with tap and
keyboard navigation. There's also a browser-based admin tool so clergy and translators can edit the
texts without touching code. On GitHub Pages it's password-protected and encrypted at deploy time.
