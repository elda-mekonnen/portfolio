## Why I built it

In the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, every day of the year has its own set of liturgy readings: a
Pauline epistle, a Catholic epistle, Acts, a Psalm, and a Gospel. A lot of people in my community,
me included, like to use that day's readings for our own daily Bible reading.

Finding them was harder than it should be. The readings live in a printed lectionary or a scanned
PDF, and the system behind them isn't obvious. Each calendar day has its readings, but seasons like
Great Lent, along with Sundays and feast days, have their own readings that take precedence. If you
don't know those rules, it's easy to end up on the wrong passage. And there's no official English
lectionary, so people who read mainly in English are left out.

I wanted one place where anyone could open today's readings, already sorted out, in the language
they read best.

## What I built

The app opens on today. A banner shows where you are in the church year, like "Great Lent · Zewerede
Sunday · Day 1", with the Gregorian and Ethiopian dates side by side. Below it are the day's five
readings.

Tap a reading and the full passage opens right in the app, in English, Amharic, or both side by
side, so you don't need a separate Bible. You can widen a passage by five verses on either side
for context.

Behind the scenes, the app works out which readings apply. The everyday calendar is the base
layer. Seasonal schedules for Great Lent, Sundays, and feast days sit on top with a priority order,
so the right readings win automatically and nobody has to know the rules.

Because you can step to any date, it also works for preparing ahead. Open the coming Sunday and you
can read its readings before the liturgy.
